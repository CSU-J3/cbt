The dashboard rework is the first application, not the deliverable. These eight rules are. Each pair below is real CBT content — the left panel is the shape that ships today on at least one route, the right is the shape the arc lands on. The dashed markers measure themselves on resize using the same rule M1 applies in the audit, so this page reads differently at 1440 than at 2560 — which is the point of C1 and C2 both.

C1No far-right anchors in a row

A row must not pin an element to its right edge while its content packs left. Rows are flex, packed left; leftover width collects at the end where it's invisible. Highest-yield rule in the set — every other convention is cheaper to apply once this one lands.

C2Type scales with the viewport

One root value, one step ladder, row padding and fixed glyph strips stepping at the same breakpoints. 12px on a 2560px monitor is unreadable and no amount of layout fixes that. The unit is rem against an unclaimed root, not em — em compounds through nesting, and this stylesheet nests deeply.

C3Color carries meaning, not decoration

One bright element per row — its title. Borders and color are reserved for state and interaction. Only stage is colored, because stage is what a reader scans for. This refines the HO 465–469 chip family rather than replacing it: chips stay for filters and interactive affordances, and become plain dim text everywhere they were labels.

C6Density floor — not a density target

12px base, rows ≥22px, full-width hover targets. Past this, further tightening costs legibility rather than saving space. Written as a floor because the rework's direction is more content per screen, and a floor is what stops that from becoming its own defect.

C8Wide screens buy columns, not wider rows

Past roughly 900px a row of text gains nothing from more width — it just pushes its right-hand element further away, which is C1 again. A max-width cap throws the monitor away. Add panes instead.

Three of eight have no instrument. That's deliberate and it's the honest read: C5 and C6 are review rules, and C8 is C1 seen from the other end. The audit table therefore prices four conventions, not eight — so P3–P5 are "done" on M1/M2/M3/M4 reaching zero, and C5/C6 ride the per-route eyeball pass that the table exists to keep short.
