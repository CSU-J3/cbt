The marks are drawn, not described. One canvas function draws every icon from the mark, the ground and the size, in IBM Plex Mono 600 (the masthead's face) and CT's own colours. The 16 and 32px views under the tab strip are those pixels, blown up. At 32px and under each mark is cut about a fifth larger, as tab icons usually are, and the dome's lines are thicker; the phones and the sheet's 128 and 48 keep the full margin. The handoff would render the ruled mark the same way to PNG files with the self-hosted font, so no device needs the font.

The phones are sketches. The wallpapers, the three grey apps and their names are invented. Android cuts each icon to its launcher's shape and only promises to keep the middle 80% (the maskable icon's safe zone); the Pixel's default is a circle, drawn here. An iPhone keeps the whole square and rounds its corners.

The Fold frames are half size of your open screen (851 × 883). The status bar, Chrome's bar and the gesture strip are the 60, 70 and 32px read off your Nov 13 screenshot for mock-817. The page under them is a dim sketch of the dashboard's masthead; the splash layout is a sketch of Chrome's.

What makes it installable. Chrome offers Install app once the manifest has a name, a 192px and a 512px icon, a start URL and a display of standalone, fullscreen or minimal-ui (web.dev, install criteria). No service worker is on that list. With browser, Chrome's menu still adds a shortcut, which opens a tab.

iPhones decide for themselves now. Since iOS 26, a site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app by default, and the person adding it can turn Open as Web App off. So on an iPhone the display setting matters less than the icon and the name.
